Week 5: Integration & Reflection

Christlike Attributes, Usability Testing, Ethical Reflection, and Spiritual-Professional Integration

Task 1: Christlike Attributes & Personal Warning Signs

Comprehensive Review & Reflection

As I approach the finalization of my portfolio, I have conducted a thorough review of how Christlike attributes are woven into my personal and professional life. The Savior's counsel in Luke 21:19 (King James Version) — "In your patience possess ye your souls" — has become a guiding principle during this iterative development process. Patience, I have learned, is not passive waiting but active trust in God's timing while I refine my work with diligence and care.

Christlike Attributes I Am Integrating

Patience

Trusting God's timing in portfolio development, career progression, and relationships. Not rushing perfection but pursuing it steadily.

Humility

Seeking feedback on my work, admitting when I need help, and recognizing that my talents are stewardships from God, not self-made achievements.

Charity

Using my IT skills to serve others — protecting patient data, mentoring youth in technology, and building systems that uplift rather than exclude.

Personal Warning Signs & Realignment Strategies

Warning Sign 1: Procrastination Disguised as "Waiting for Inspiration"

Recognition: I notice I delay starting complex tasks (like this portfolio) by telling myself I need to "feel ready" or "wait for the right moment." This is often fear of imperfection masquerading as patience.

Realignment Strategy: I commit to the "five-minute rule" — start any task for just five minutes. Usually, momentum carries me forward. I also pray before beginning work, inviting the Holy Ghost to guide my effort, remembering President Nelson's counsel that "the Lord loves effort."

Warning Sign 2: Isolation When Overwhelmed

Recognition: When coursework or work pressures mount, my instinct is to withdraw — stop attending study groups, delay responding to messages, and try to solve everything alone. This leads to burnout and missed opportunities for collaboration.

Realignment Strategy: I have identified two "accountability partners" — a classmate and a mentor — whom I commit to contacting weekly, even when I feel I have nothing to share. I also attend the temple regularly, where the peace of covenants reminds me I am not alone in my struggles.

Warning Sign 3: Pride in Technical Knowledge

Recognition: As I gain IT expertise, I sometimes catch myself dismissing others' questions as "basic" or feeling superior when I solve problems quickly. This contradicts the charity Christ commands.

Realignment Strategy: I remind myself of Alma 37:6 — "by small and simple things are great things brought to pass." Every expert was once a beginner. I commit to mentoring at least one person each month, teaching with patience and remembering that knowledge is a stewardship to be shared, not hoarded.

This review will guide the finalization of my portfolio, ensuring that every section reflects not just technical competence but the Christlike character I strive to develop.

Task 2: Usability Testing & Design Enhancements

Usability Testing Methodology

I conducted usability testing on my portfolio between July 20–25, 2026, using three methods:

  • Peer Navigation Test: Asked two classmates to navigate the site without guidance and note any confusion.
  • Mobile Responsiveness Check: Tested on iPhone 13 (Safari) and Samsung Galaxy A54 (Chrome) to verify layout integrity.
  • Accessibility Audit: Used keyboard-only navigation (Tab key) and checked color contrast ratios using WebAIM's contrast checker.

Issues Identified & Fixes Implemented

Issue Severity Fix Implemented Impact
Mobile hamburger menu did not close after tapping a link High Added JavaScript event listener to close menu on link click Improved mobile navigation flow; users no longer need to manually close menu
Week 4 video placeholder link was non-functional High Embedded Google Drive video player and added fallback button Video now plays inline; instructor can view without leaving site
Footer Quick Links on About, Skills, Projects, Contact pages were incomplete Medium Standardized footer across all pages to include all six navigation links Consistent navigation; users can reach any page from any footer
Color contrast on light-gray text (#6b7280) over white background was borderline (4.4:1) Medium Darkened body text color to #4b5563 (contrast ratio 7.5:1) Meets WCAG AA standard; improved readability for visually impaired users
No visible focus indicator on navigation links for keyboard users Low Added CSS :focus-visible outline to all interactive elements Keyboard navigation now meets accessibility standards

Overall Impact

These enhancements improved the portfolio's usability score (self-assessed against Nielsen's 10 heuristics) from 7/10 to 9/10. The site is now fully navigable on mobile devices, accessible via keyboard, and provides clear visual feedback on all interactive elements. The embedded video and standardized footer create a more cohesive, professional user experience.

Ethical Dilemma Reflection: Future Application

Revisiting the EMR Access Dilemma

In Week 2, I analyzed the situation where a senior nurse accessed patient records outside her authorization. In Week 4, I proposed an alternative approach: document objectively, report to compliance, and offer the nurse a path to self-report. Now, in Week 5, I reflect on how this exercise shapes my future professional conduct.

My View on the Dilemma

I believe the nurse's actions, while perhaps motivated by genuine concern, violated the foundational ethical principle of patient autonomy. Patients entrust healthcare systems with their most sensitive information under the implicit promise that access will be restricted to those directly involved in their care. When that trust is breached — even with good intentions — it erodes the entire system's credibility. As an IT professional managing such systems, I am not merely a technician; I am a guardian of that trust.

How I Will Handle Similar Situations in Future Work

  • Immediate Documentation: I will objectively log any anomalous access patterns (user ID, timestamp, records accessed) without confronting the individual first. This preserves evidence and prevents emotional escalation.
  • Report Through Proper Channels: I will report to the designated Compliance Officer or Privacy Official, as mandated by organizational policy and HIPAA-like regulations. I will not attempt to "handle it myself" or cover it up to protect a colleague's reputation.
  • Offer Restorative Pathways: Where appropriate, I will encourage the individual to self-report to the ethics committee, framing it as an opportunity for accountability and growth rather than punitive destruction. This balances justice with Christlike compassion.
  • Advocate for Systemic Safeguards: I will use such incidents as evidence to advocate for stronger technical controls (e.g., automated audit logging, role-based access control) so that future breaches are prevented, not just punished.

What This Exercise Taught Me

This multi-week analysis taught me that ethical challenges in IT are rarely black-and-white. The nurse was not a villain; she was a caring professional who made a poor decision under emotional pressure. My role as an IT leader is not to judge her character but to protect the system's integrity while treating her with dignity. This exercise also reinforced that ethical principles (honesty, responsibility, justice) are not abstract ideals — they are operational requirements that must be embedded into system design, access policies, and incident response protocols. As I advance in my career, I will carry this understanding into every database I design, every access control I implement, and every policy I help write.

Reflective Narrative: How Faith Influences My Professional Endeavors

This week, I attended the temple and sat in the quiet of the celestial room, pondering the question: How does my faith actually change the way I work? The answer came not as a thunderclap but as a gentle recognition: my faith does not merely accompany my professional life; it authors it.

When I verify a patient's medical record, I am not just checking a database entry — I am honoring the divine worth of that individual, remembering that they are a child of God whose privacy is sacred. When I design an access control system, I am not just writing code — I am building a wall of protection around the vulnerable, fulfilling the covenant I made to "mourn with those that mourn" (Mosiah 18:9). When I mentor a young person in technology, I am not just transferring skills — I am multiplying the talents God entrusted to me, ensuring they are not buried in the ground (Matthew 25:14–30).

Elder Bednar taught that the gospel is not a checklist of discrete tasks but a "magnificent tapestry of truth fitly framed and woven together" (Bednar, 2018). I see this tapestry in my own life: the strand of faith gives me courage to take on challenging projects; the strand of integrity ensures I do not cut corners when no one is watching; the strand of stewardship drives me to keep learning even when I am tired; the strand of service reminds me that my skills exist to bless others, not to inflate my résumé; and the strand of patience — perhaps the hardest — teaches me to trust that God's timing is perfect, even when I want to rush ahead.

These strands, woven together around the center of Jesus Christ, form a rope strong enough to withstand the winds of opposition, the pressures of deadlines, and the temptations of pride. This is the rope I carry into every workplace, every classroom, and every conversation. It is, in the truest sense, who I am.
