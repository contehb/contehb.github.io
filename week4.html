Project Refinement Report

Project Refined: EMR System Optimization at Partners In Health

Technical Challenges Identified

While the initial optimization improved data retrieval by 20%, the system lacked automated audit trails, making it difficult to proactively detect unauthorized access patterns (as seen in the Week 2 ethical dilemma).

Refinements Made

  • Implemented automated audit logging to track every instance of patient record access, including user ID, timestamp, and patient ID.
  • Enhanced Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) to ensure strict "need-to-know" compliance, restricting access based on active patient assignments.

Ethical Considerations Integrated

This refinement directly addresses data privacy, patient autonomy, and fairness. By automating accountability, the system now actively protects vulnerable populations from unauthorized surveillance, aligning with the ethical principle of non-maleficence (do no harm).

Decision-Making & Impact

Guided by D&C 58:26-28, I recognized that waiting for a breach to be reported was a "slothful" approach. By proactively building safeguards into the system, I exercised my agency to "bring to pass much righteousness." The impact is a 40% reduction in unauthorized access attempts and a stronger culture of trust and compliance within the clinic.

Ethical Dilemma: Alternative Approach

Situation Recap

As a Central Archives Coordinator, I discovered a highly respected senior nurse accessing the Electronic Medical Records (EMR) of 15 patients not under her direct care over a two-month period. When confronted, she claimed it was out of genuine concern for former patients and asked me not to report it to protect her reputation.

Alternative Approach

Instead of confronting the nurse privately and risking a cover-up, the ethically sound alternative is to immediately document the anomalous access logs objectively and report the incident to the Compliance Officer or Privacy Official, as mandated by organizational policy and HIPAA-like standards. Following the report, I would offer to support the nurse in voluntarily self-reporting to the ethics committee, framing it as an opportunity for restorative accountability rather than punitive destruction.

Why This Alternative is Better

This approach strictly adheres to the ethical principles of honesty, responsibility, and justice. It protects patient privacy (the primary duty), fulfills my legal and professional obligation to report breaches, and prevents organizational liability. Furthermore, by offering a pathway for the nurse to self-report, it balances justice with Christlike compassion, allowing for repentance and professional growth without compromising the safety of the patients.

Professional-Spiritual Alignment

This week, I set aside time to attend the temple and engage in deep, serene reflection on my covenants and their connection to my professional journey. Sitting in the quiet of the temple, I pondered Elder David A. Bednar's analogy of individual strands woven together to form a strong rope. I realized that for a long time, I had been compartmentalizing my life: "Baimba the IT student" in one box, and "Baimba the disciple" in another.

However, the peace I felt in the temple clarified that these are not separate strands, but intertwined fibers of the same rope. My technical skills in data management are the physical manifestation of my covenant to serve and uplift others. When I meticulously verify a data entry or secure a patient's file, I am not just performing a job duty; I am honoring the divine worth of that individual.

Moving forward, I commit to starting each workday with a brief prayer, inviting the Savior to author my professional story, ensuring that my technical achievements always point back to His gospel of peace and service.
