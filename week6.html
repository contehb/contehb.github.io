Week 6: Finalization & Presentation

Action Plan, Final Portfolios, Reflective Essay, and Peer Review Report

Detailed Action Plan for Ongoing Development

Spiritual Development

  • Daily: Morning prayer + 20 minutes of scripture study with one written insight.
  • Weekly: Sacrament renewal, family home evening, and one deliberate act of service.
  • Monthly: Temple attendance and a personal "warning signs" check-in against my three realignment strategies.
  • Ongoing: Keep a gratitude-and-guidance journal to record how the Holy Ghost directs my decisions (Hurley, n.d.).

Professional Development

  • 0–6 months: Complete current Ensign College courses at my 4.0 standard; publish this portfolio; mentor one youth in basic computer skills each month.
  • 6–18 months: Finish the B.S. in Information Technology (2027); earn an entry cybersecurity certification; add one new project case study to this site per term.
  • 2–5 years: Step into a cybersecurity or systems-administration role where I protect critical services; continue temple habits; lead teams with disciple-leadership principles (Morrison, n.d.).
  • Measure: Quarterly self-audit — skills gained, people served, covenants kept — reviewed in prayer.

Integration Habit

  • Begin each workday with a brief prayer inviting the Savior to author my professional story.
  • Treat every data record as a person and every access right as a sacred trust.
  • When the path bends unexpectedly, remember that the Lord's direction often arrives on the detour (Morrison, n.d.).
  • Keep the strands — faith, integrity, stewardship, service, patience — woven around Christ, the center (Bednar, 2018).

Final Spiritual Progression Portfolio

My final spiritual portfolio is the integrated record of three guiding principles — Faith in Jesus Christ, Unwavering Integrity, and Continuous Learning as Sacred Stewardship — lived out across this course. It is compiled from scripture (3 Nephi 13:19–22; D&C 58:26–28; D&C 88:67), prophetic counsel (Elder Bednar's rope analogy, Oct 2018), sacred media ("Jesus Christ: The Prince of Peace"; "Blessings of the Temple"), and personal temple reflection notes. Week by week, these materials moved from a list to a lifeline: patience became my project-management method, integrity became my access-control philosophy, and learning became my form of worship.

The full, detailed content lives on this site: my principles and materials on Week 4, my Christlike attributes, warning signs, and mental map on Week 5, and my journey and covenants on About.

"In your patience possess ye your souls" (Luke 21:19) is no longer a verse I admire; it is the operating system I run under pressure.

Final Professional Growth Portfolio

My final professional portfolio presents a complete IT identity: a résumé anchored by my role as Central Archives Coordinator at Partners In Health (EMR data integrity, 20% faster retrieval, strict privacy SOPs), service as Data Supervisor at Statistics Sierra Leone, and experience as an ICT instructor; verified certifications (Gemini Educator, TestOut PC Pro, Microsoft SQL Server 2022); and three case-study projects — this portfolio website, the EMR optimization, and a normalized SQL inventory system — each pairing a measurable outcome with the value it was built on.

Across Weeks 2–6 the portfolio evolved from a showcase into a stewardship record: usability-tested (Week 5), accessibility-hardened, ethically annotated, and fully cross-linked. The complete detail lives on Projects, Skills, and Home.

Alignment check: my initial goal was to "show my skills." My final goal is to "protect people and bless communities with my skills." The target moved from me to others — and that is the growth this course produced.

Reflective Essay: Alignment and Evolution of My Goals

When I began IT 497, my goal was straightforward: build a website that proves I can do the work. Six weeks later, I can see that the course quietly re-aimed that goal. The portfolio I submit today still proves competence — semantic HTML, responsive CSS, accessible markup, a live GitHub Pages deployment — but its deeper argument is different. It claims that a data record is a person, that an access right is a trust, and that a line of code is a small act of stewardship. That shift, from proof-of-skill to proof-of-stewardship, is the true deliverable of this course.

The evolution was not smooth, and honesty requires naming the adjustments. Early on I treated the spiritual and professional sections as two folders in the same cabinet. Elder Bednar's rope analogy (Bednar, 2018) exposed that segmentation: separate strands do not hold weight. So I rewrote the narrative to interweave them — my EMR audit-logging refinement became an expression of integrity, my accessibility work became an expression of charity, my patience with broken deployments became an exercise in Luke 21:19. Technical setbacks I once read as failures I now read as the "unexpected path" where direction actually arrives (Morrison, n.d.), and the effort itself became part of the worship (Hurley, n.d.).

Practically, the portfolio changed shape each week in response to testing and feedback: a working video replaced a dead link, footers were standardized so no page is a dead end, a references section was added to honor APA, and the usability report put severity and impact next to every fix. Each adjustment was small; together they turned a document into a place — a place a recruiter can navigate, a grader can verify, and a disciple can recognize himself in.

Looking forward, this portfolio becomes a living instrument: the URL I hand to employers, the record I audit quarterly against my action plan, and the testimony I leave my family of what I believed and how I worked. My goals did not shrink from their original ambition; they grew a soul.
